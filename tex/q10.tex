  \begin{abstract}
    Papers Q7\textendash Q9 of the Cosmochrony spectral geometry programme establish that the effective
    operator $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ on $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \Heis_3(\mathbb{R})$ has a principal symbol
    $\sigma_2(L_{\mathrm{eff}})|_{H_{\mathrm{eff}}} = A_H(k_X^2+k_Y^2) + A_Z k_Z^2$ with $A_H, A_Z > 0$
    and $A_Z = 2 = \Csu$ (Casimir eigenvalue on $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$, Q8).
    The identification $A_H = 2$ would complete the effective metric to $g^{\mu\nu} =
    \mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, 2, 2, 2)$ and establish the isotropic Lorentzian geometry of the
    Cosmochrony framework.
    The present paper proves $A_H \to 2$ from two structural inputs: (i) the asymptotic
    character-independence of the O-series spectral observables
    ($\sigma_c(n) \to \sigma_*(n)$ uniformly in $c$ as $q \to \infty$, supported structurally by the
    conjugate-pair equality $\sigma_c = \sigma_{q-c}$ (the conjugation identity, O17; recalled in
    O18 Theorem~3.1)~\cite{Beau2026a21,Beau2026a22} and confirmed numerically by the O25
    campaign~\cite{Beau2026a29}); and (ii) the uniqueness of the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-invariant
    quadratic form on $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$ (Q7 Lemma~4.3~\cite{Beau2026q7}).
    The core argument is that character-independence forces the effective form on $\Heff$ to be
    scalar under the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ action, and the unique such form is the Casimir with value 2.
    The result is established conditionally on the O-series universality (structurally supported,
    numerically confirmed for $q \leq 211$) and the bridge non-obstruction hypothesis of Q7\textendash Q9.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}

    \subsection{The remaining open problem}

      The chain of results Q5a\textendash Q9~\cite{Beau2026q5a,Beau2026q5b,Beau2026q7} reduces the
      geometry of the Cosmochrony effective spacetime to a single open question:
      does the horizontal coefficient $A_H$ of the effective spatial co-metric equal the central
      coefficient $A_Z = 2$?
      More precisely, Q7\textendash Q8 establish:
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:symbol}
        \sigma_2(L_{\mathrm{eff}})\big|_{\Heff}
        = A_H(q)\bigl(k_X^2 + k_Y^2\bigr) + 2\,k_Z^2,
        \qquad A_H(q) > 0,\quad A_Z = 2,
      \end{equation}
      where $A_H(q)$ is determined numerically and $A_Z = \Csu|_{\Sym^2(V_\rho)} = 2$ is fixed
      algebraically~\cite{BeauQ8}.
      The bridge conjecture (Q7 Conjecture~4.7,~\cite{Beau2026q7}) is equivalent, given Q7\textendash Q9,
      to the question:
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:isotropy}
        A_H(q) \;\xrightarrow{q\to\infty}\; 2.
      \end{equation}
      Numerical evidence from Q7 (Table~1) gives
      $|A_H(q) - 2| \approx 0.264\,q^{-0.44}$ for $q \in \{61, 101, 151\}$,
      supporting~\eqref{eq:isotropy};
      the present paper provides the structural argument for this convergence.
    \subsection{Main result}

      \begin{theorem}[Asymptotic isotropy $A_H \to 2$]\label{thm:main}
        Assume the O-series spectral universality \textup{[U]} (Definition~\ref{def:universality})
        as a convergence statement: $\varepsilon(q) \to 0$.
        Items~\textup{(i)}\textendash\textup{(iii)} of Proposition~\ref{prop:structural-U} provide
        structural support for~\textup{[U]}; item~\textup{(iv)} provides numerical evidence.
        Under~\textup{[U]} and the Q5a hypotheses \textup{[H1]}, \textup{[H2]}, \textup{[H-w]},
        \textup{[H-E1]}, \textup{[C]}, the horizontal coefficient satisfies
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
          \lim_{q\to\infty} A_H(q) = 2.
        \end{equation}
        More precisely, $|A_H(q) - 2| \leq C\,\varepsilon(q)$ for a constant $C > 0$ independent of $q$.
      \end{theorem}

      The value $\alpha$ is estimated numerically as $\approx 0.44$ from O25
      (Section~\ref{sec:numerical}).
      Together with Q7\textendash Q9, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} establishes the isotropic effective
      metric $g^{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, 2, 2, 2)$ and resolves Case~1 of the bridge
      conjecture.

    \subsection{Structure of the argument}

      The proof runs through three stages.
      First (Section~\ref{sec:universality}), we establish that the O-series spectral observables
      $\sigma_c(n)$ become character-independent as $q \to \infty$: all characters
      $c \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$ produce the same fingerprint energy profile.
      Second (Section~\ref{sec:su2}), we show that character-independence implies that the effective
      quadratic form on $\Heff = \Sym^2(V_\rho)$ is asymptotically invariant under the
      $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ action.
      Third (Section~\ref{sec:casimir}), we use the uniqueness of the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-invariant
      quadratic form on the spin-1 representation (Casimir, value 2) to conclude $A_H \to 2$.

    \subsection{Organisation}

      Section~\ref{sec:setup} recalls the relevant results of Q7\textendash Q9 and fixes notation.
      Section~\ref{sec:universality} states and establishes the O-series universality hypothesis~[U].
      Section~\ref{sec:su2} derives the asymptotic $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-isotropy of the effective form.
      Section~\ref{sec:casimir} completes the identification $A_H = 2$.
      Section~\ref{sec:numerical} records the numerical confirmation from the O25 campaign.
      Section~\ref{sec:consequences} draws the programme-level consequences.

  \section{Setup and Recall}\label{sec:setup}

    \subsection{The effective operator and its symbol}

      Let $L_{\mathrm{eff}} = -A_\tau\partial_\tau^2 + A_H\,\DHeis$ be the effective operator on
      $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \Heis_3(\mathbb{R})$, established conditionally in Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b}
      and unconditionally (under Q5a hypotheses) by Q9~\cite{Beau2026q9}.
      The effective subspace is $\Heff = \Sym^2(V_\rho)$, where $V_\rho$ is the spin-$\frac{1}{2}$
      representation of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ and $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$ is the spin-1 representation
      with weights $m \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ and spanning vectors $\{e_-, e_0, e_+\}$.
      By Q7 Proposition~6.1~\cite{Beau2026q7} (no cross terms) and Q8 (Casimir rigidity,~\cite{BeauQ8}),
      the restricted symbol takes the form~\eqref{eq:symbol} with $A_Z = 2$ and $A_H(q) > 0$
      (Q9 Corollary~5.2,~\cite{Beau2026q9}).

    \subsection{O-series spectral observables}

      The O-series~\cite{Beau2026a26,Beau2026a27,Beau2026a28,Beau2026a29} defines, for each prime
      $q$, character $c \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$, and BFS depth $n$, the normalised
      Weil fingerprint energy:
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:sigmac}
        \sigma_c(n) := \frac{\delta r_n}{|S_n|},
      \end{equation}
      where $\delta r_n$ is the spectral radius increment and $|S_n|$ is the shell size at depth~$n$.
      The pair observable is
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:sigmapair}
        \spair(n) := \sigma_c(n)\cdot\sigma_{q-c}(n),
      \end{equation}
      with exponent $\dpair$ defined by $\spair(n) \sim n^{-\dpair}$ over the fitting window
      $[n_0, n_1]$.
      The BI-parity symmetry (O22~\cite{Beau2026a26}):
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:biparity}
        \sigma_c(n) = \sigma_{q-c}(n) \qquad \text{for all } c \text{ and all } n,
      \end{equation}
      holds structurally and is confirmed to numerical precision across all tested primes.

    \subsection{The weight\textendash character correspondence}

      The bridge identification of Q7~\cite{Beau2026q7} maps the spin weights of $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$
      to O-series character sectors:
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:weight-char}
        m = +1 \;\leftrightarrow\; c \in (0, q/2), \quad
        m = 0 \;\leftrightarrow\; c \in [q/2-w, q/2+w], \quad
        m = -1 \;\leftrightarrow\; c \in (q/2, q).
      \end{equation}
      This correspondence is the content of Q7 Remark~4.2~\cite{Beau2026q7}: the three spin sectors
      of $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$ (the horizontal $e_\pm$ and central $e_0$ directions) are identified with
      the character sectors of the Weil fingerprint through the bridge $\Vphi$.
      The central sector $m = 0$ is identified with a symmetric window of characters of the form
      $c \in [q/2 - w, q/2 + w]$ for a fixed width $w \geq 1$ independent of $q$; its contribution
      under the uniform bound of~[U] is therefore comparable to that of any other character, since
      $|\sigma_c(n) - \sigma_*(n)| \leq \varepsilon(q)\sigma_*(n)$ holds uniformly in $c$.
      Under this identification, the coefficient $A_H(q)$ measures how the fingerprint energy varies
      across the $m = \pm 1$ sectors relative to the $m = 0$ sector ($A_Z = 2$).

  \section{O-Series Universality}\label{sec:universality}

    \subsection{The universality hypothesis}

      \begin{definition}[O-series spectral universality, {\upshape [U]}]\label{def:universality}
        The O-series satisfies spectral universality~\textup{[U]} if the fingerprint energy $\sigma_c(n)$
        becomes character-independent as $q \to \infty$: there exists a function $\sigma_*(n)$ such that
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:universality}
          \max_{c \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times}
          \bigl|\sigma_c(n) - \sigma_*(n)\bigr|
          \;\leq\; \varepsilon(q)\,\sigma_*(n),
        \end{equation}
        where $\varepsilon(q) \to 0$ as $q \to \infty$, uniformly over $n \leq n^*(q)$.
      \end{definition}

      The function $\sigma_*(n)$ is the universal power law $\sigma_*(n) = C_*\,n^{-\dpair/2}$
      with $\dpair \approx 7.44$ (O-series canonical value).

    \subsection{Structural support for \textup{[U]}}

      \begin{proposition}[Structural support for \textup{[U]}]\label{prop:structural-U}
        The O-series universality hypothesis~\textup{[U]} holds in the following structural sense.

        \begin{enumerate}
          \item[(i)] \textup{(BI-parity, O22~\cite{Beau2026a26}).}
          The symmetry $\sigma_c(n) = \sigma_{q-c}(n)$ holds unconditionally.
          Consequently, the $m = +1$ and $m = -1$ sectors are exactly equal:
          $\sigma_c = \sigma_{q-c}$ for all $c$ and $q$.

          \item[(ii)] \textup{(Supplied selection rule, O23~\cite{Beau2026a27}).}
          The pair structure $\Sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ (three stable directions) is a supplied
          selection rule: O23 proves the three-dimensionality of the neutral sector of a
          supplied spinor carrier (Theorem~3.1), while the carrier selection and the
          identification of $\Sigma_c$ with that dimension are open bridges.
          Conditionally on that carrier, this fixes the structure of $\sigma_*(n)$ within the
          fitting window.

          \item[(iii)] \textup{(Rank stability, O24~\cite{Beau2026a28}).}
          The observable rank is stable across conjugate pairs $(c, q-c)$ with the same exponent
          $\dpair(c) = \dpair(q-c)$ for all tested primes $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151\}$.

          \item[(iv)] \textup{(Numerical universality, O25~\cite{Beau2026a29}).}
          The per-pair exponents satisfy $\Var_c[\dpair(c)] \to 0$ as $q \to \infty$:
          inter-pair standard deviation decreases from $0.54$ ($q=29$) to $0.16$ ($q=101$).
        \end{enumerate}
      \end{proposition}

      \begin{proof}
        Item~(i) is proved in O22~\cite{Beau2026a26}.
        Item~(ii) is conditional: O23~\cite{Beau2026a27} proves it for a supplied spinor carrier
        (Theorem~3.1), and $\Sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ enters as a supplied selection rule.
        Item~(iii) is established in O24~\cite{Beau2026a28}.
        Item~(iv) is the main numerical result of O25~\cite{Beau2026a29}:
        the inter-pair variance of $\dpair$ is reported as $\sigma = 0.54$ at $q=29$, $\sigma = 0.26$
        at $q=61$, $\sigma = 0.16$ at $q=101$, decreasing monotonically.
      \end{proof}

      \begin{remark}[Scope of \textup{[U]}]\label{rem:U-scope}
        Items~(i) and~(iii) of Proposition~\ref{prop:structural-U} establish~[U] at the
        structural level for the sectors $m = \pm 1$ vs.\ $m = 0$; item~(ii) contributes
        conditionally on the supplied rank-three carrier.
        Item~(iv) provides quantitative confirmation that the remaining inter-pair variance vanishes.
        The full proof of~\eqref{eq:universality} for general $c$ — covering sectors beyond the
        canonical BI-parity pairing — is the content of the O25 large-prime extension
        ($q=211$ confirmed: $A_H(211)=2.022$, Stage~C PASS; $q\in\{307,401\}$ pending).
      \end{remark}

  \section{Asymptotic $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-Isotropy}\label{sec:su2}

    \subsection{Character-independence implies $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-invariance}

      \begin{lemma}[Asymptotic $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-isotropy]\label{lem:su2-isotropy}
        Under the O-series universality~\textup{[U]} and the weight\textendash character correspondence
        \eqref{eq:weight-char}, the effective quadratic form
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:Q-form}
          Q_q := A_H(q)(k_X^2 + k_Y^2) + A_Z k_Z^2
        \end{equation}
        is asymptotically $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-invariant as $q \to \infty$:
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:anisotropy}
          |A_H(q) - A_Z| \;\leq\; C'\,\varepsilon(q),
        \end{equation}
        where $\varepsilon(q) \to 0$ is the universality rate of~\textup{[U]} and $C' > 0$ is a
        constant independent of $q$.
      \end{lemma}

      \begin{proof}
        We relate the anisotropy $|A_H - A_Z|$ to the character variance $\varepsilon(q)$ through
        three steps.

        \textbf{Step~1: Anisotropy measures character variance.}
        The coefficient $A_H(q)$ is determined by the effective form restricted to the $m = \pm 1$
        sectors (horizontal), and $A_Z$ by the $m = 0$ sector (central).
        This identification follows from Q7 Criterion~5.1~\cite{Beau2026q7}, which relates the symbol
        coefficients of $\sigma_2(L_{\mathrm{eff}})$ to the fingerprint energy sector-wise: the
        coefficient of $k_X^2 + k_Y^2$ equals the admissibility weight in the $m = \pm 1$ spin sector,
        and the coefficient of $k_Z^2$ equals the weight in the $m = 0$ sector.
        We do not re-derive this identification here, but note that it is the content of Q7 §5.1 and
        depends only on the bridge non-obstruction established in Q7\textendash Q9 (not on the
        full bridge conjecture).

        Under the weight\textendash character correspondence~\eqref{eq:weight-char}, the ratio $A_H/A_Z$
        therefore equals the ratio of fingerprint energy averaged over $m = \pm 1$ characters to the
        energy at $m = 0$.
        Note that since the symbol coefficients depend linearly on the admissibility weights
        (Q7 Criterion~5.1~\cite{Beau2026q7}), sector-wise averages of $\sigma_c$ directly determine
        $A_H$ and $A_Z$, and the ratio is well-defined and finite for all $q$:
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio}
          \frac{A_H(q)}{A_Z}
          = \frac{E_c[\sigma_c(n) : c \in m{=}\pm 1\text{ sector}]}{E_c[\sigma_c(n) : c \in [q/2-w,\,q/2+w]]}.
        \end{equation}

        \textbf{Step~2: Universality bounds the ratio.}
        Under~[U]: $|\sigma_c(n) - \sigma_*(n)| \leq \varepsilon(q)\sigma_*(n)$ for all $c$ and
        uniformly over the fitting window $n \leq n_*(q)$ (Definition~\ref{def:universality}).
        Therefore:
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio-bound}
          \left|\frac{E_c[\sigma_c]}{E_{c'}[\sigma_{c'}]} - 1\right|
          \;\leq\; 2\,\varepsilon(q)
        \end{equation}
        for any two character averages $E_c$, $E_{c'}$ (since both are within $\varepsilon(q)$ of
        $\sigma_*$).
        Applying~\eqref{eq:ratio-bound} to the ratio in~\eqref{eq:ratio}:
        \begin{equation}
          \left|\frac{A_H(q)}{A_Z} - 1\right| \;\leq\; 2\,\varepsilon(q).
        \end{equation}

        \textbf{Step~3: Absolute bound.}
        Since $A_Z = 2$ (Q8), multiplying through by $A_Z = 2$ gives:
        \begin{equation}
          |A_H(q) - 2| \;\leq\; 4\,\varepsilon(q) =: C'\,\varepsilon(q).
        \end{equation}
        As $\varepsilon(q) \to 0$ by hypothesis~[U], we conclude $A_H(q) \to A_Z = 2$.
      \end{proof}

      \begin{remark}[Role of BI parity]\label{rem:biparity-role}
        Proposition~\ref{prop:structural-U}(i) establishes that the $m = +1$ and $m = -1$ sectors are
        exactly equal ($\sigma_c = \sigma_{q-c}$) for all finite $q$.
        This means the form~\eqref{eq:Q-form} is already isotropic in the horizontal plane: the
        coefficients of $k_X^2$ and $k_Y^2$ are exactly equal for all $q$.
        The remaining anisotropy $A_H(q) \neq A_Z$ is a vertical\textendash horizontal asymmetry
        (between $m = 0$ and $m = \pm 1$), which is driven by the finite-$q$ corrections to the
        universal power law $\sigma_*(n)$.
        As these corrections vanish by~[U], the full $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-isotropy is recovered.
      \end{remark}

  \section{Casimir Identification and Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}\label{sec:casimir}

    \subsection{Uniqueness of the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-invariant form}

      \begin{proposition}[Casimir uniqueness on $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$]\label{prop:casimir-unique}
        The space $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$ carries the spin-1 irreducible representation of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$.
        The unique \textup{(}up to positive scalar\textup{)} $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-invariant positive-definite
        quadratic form on $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$ is the Casimir:
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:casimir}
          \Csu\big|_{\Sym^2(V_\rho)} = 2\cdot\mathrm{Id}.
        \end{equation}
        In particular, the unique $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-invariant co-metric with spatial eigenvalue $A_Z = 2$
        is $g_{\mathrm{sp}} = 2(k_X^2 + k_Y^2 + k_Z^2) = \Csu \cdot (dk_X^2 + dk_Y^2 + dk_Z^2)$.
      \end{proposition}

      \begin{proof}
        By Schur's lemma, the space of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-equivariant endomorphisms of an irreducible
        representation is one-dimensional (scalar multiples of the identity).
        Every $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-invariant quadratic form $Q$ on $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$ is associated to such
        an endomorphism and is therefore a scalar multiple of the Casimir form.
        The Casimir $C_{\mathfrak{su}(2)}$ acts on the spin-$j$ representation with eigenvalue $j(j+1)$.
        For spin $j = 1$ ($\Sym^2(V_\rho)$): $C_{\mathfrak{su}(2)}|_{\Sym^2(V_\rho)} = 1\cdot 2 = 2$.
        This was established unconditionally in Q8~\cite{BeauQ8}.
      \end{proof}

    \subsection{Proof of the main theorem}

      \begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:su2-isotropy}, under~[U]:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
  |A_H(q) - A_Z| \;\leq\; C'\,\varepsilon(q) \to 0.
\end{equation}
Since $A_Z = 2$ (Q8, Proposition~\ref{prop:casimir-unique}):
\begin{equation}
  |A_H(q) - 2| \;\leq\; C'\,\varepsilon(q) \to 0.
\end{equation}
The rate $\varepsilon(q) = O(q^{-\alpha})$ is bounded from the universality rate~[U];
numerically $\alpha \approx 0.44$ (Section~\ref{sec:numerical}, Table~\ref{tab:o25}).

For the uniqueness part: once $A_H = A_Z = 2$, the effective spatial form
$2(k_X^2+k_Y^2+k_Z^2)$ is $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-invariant and equals the Casimir form by
Proposition~\ref{prop:casimir-unique}.
By Q7 Lemma~4.3~\cite{Beau2026q7} (rigidity: the bridge $\Vphi$ is unique up to positive
scalar), this Casimir form uniquely identifies the bridge as the isometric $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-equivariant
isomorphism $\Vphi : \Heff \xrightarrow{\sim} W_{\mathrm{sp}}$ of Q7 Conjecture~4.7~\cite{Beau2026q7}.
      \end{proof}

      \begin{remark}[Epistemic status]\label{rem:epistemic}
        Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is conditional on~[U], which is:
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item[(a)] proved structurally for the BI-parity sectors ($m=+1$ vs.\ $m=-1$, exact equality
          for all $q$);
          \item[(b)] conditional for the three-direction count: $\Sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ is a supplied
          selection rule, proved by O23 only for a supplied spinor carrier (Theorem~3.1);
          \item[(c)] confirmed numerically for $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151, 211\}$ with strictly
          decreasing inter-pair standard deviation
          $\sigma_q \in \{0.539, 0.259, 0.161, 0.139, 0.109\}$ (O25~\cite{Beau2026a29});
          \item[(d)] open in full generality (complete analytical proof of~\eqref{eq:universality} for
          the rate $\varepsilon(q) \to 0$).
        \end{enumerate}
        The convergence $A_H(q) \to 2$ is therefore established at the same level of confidence as the
        O-series spectral universality: structurally supported and numerically confirmed, with the
        full analytical proof identified as the remaining open step.
      \end{remark}

    The O25 campaign has since extended the dataset to $q = 211$
    ($\Var_c[\dpair]^{1/2} = 0.109$, 105 pairs, $M = 50$~\cite{Beau2026a29}),
    consistent with the monotone decrease.
    Runs for $q \in \{307, 401\}$ remain in progress and will allow
    discrimination between the exponent $\alpha = 0.44$ and $\alpha = 1/2$.

  \section{Consequences}\label{sec:consequences}

    \subsection{Completion of the effective metric}

      \begin{corollary}[Isotropic Lorentzian metric]\label{cor:metric}
        Under~\textup{[U]} and the Q5a hypotheses, the effective co-metric on
        $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \Heis_3(\mathbb{R})$ is
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:metric}
          g^{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau,\, 2,\, 2,\, 2),
        \end{equation}
        of signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and Lorentzian, with all three spatial eigenvalues equal to the
        $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-Casimir eigenvalue on $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$.
      \end{corollary}

      \begin{proof}
        This is Theorem~\ref{thm:main} combined with Q5b Theorem~6.1~\cite{Beau2026q5b} (Lorentzian
        signature) and Q8~\cite{BeauQ8} ($A_Z = 2$).
      \end{proof}

    \subsection{Bridge conjecture: closure}

      Corollary~\ref{cor:metric} completes the closure of Q7 Conjecture~4.7~\cite{Beau2026q7}.
      Under~[U], the effective symbol satisfies Q7 Criterion~5.1 with $A_H = A_Z = 2$: the
      equivariant bridge $\Vphi : \Heff \xrightarrow{\sim} W_{\mathrm{sp}}$ exists and is isometric
      with respect to the Casimir inner product.
      The bridge conjecture is therefore resolved in the isotropic case (Q7 Remark~6.8, Case~1),
      conditionally on~[U].

    \subsection{The physical interpretation}

      The identification $A_H = A_Z = 2 = \Csu$ carries a structural message:
      \emph{the effective spatial geometry of the Cosmochrony framework is not an artefact of a
      specific choice of normalisation, but is forced by the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ symmetry of the
      admissible fibre and the representation-theoretic identity $\Csu|_{\mathrm{spin}\text{-}1} = 2$}.
      Isotropy is not postulated; it is selected by the dynamics.

    \subsection{Open direction: full proof of \textup{[U]}}

      The remaining analytical task is to prove~[U] in full generality, i.e., to establish the rate
      $\varepsilon(q) \to 0$ from the structure of the O-series rather than from numerical evidence.
      The natural approach, following the O25 programme, is to:
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item Bound the inter-pair variance $\Var_c[\dpair(c)]$ by the convergence rate of the
        BFS geometry to the Carnot\textendash Carath\'eodory geometry of $\Heis_3(\mathbb{R})$
        (Q5b Theorem~3.2~\cite{Beau2026q5b}).
        \item Show that this convergence rate is $O(q^{-1/2})$ from the Bass\textendash Guivarc'h
        polynomial ball growth (Q5b Theorem~2.1~\cite{Beau2026q5b}).
        \item Conclude $\varepsilon(q) = O(q^{-1/2})$, giving $|A_H - 2| = O(q^{-1/2})$.
      \end{enumerate}
      This programme would also determine the precise exponent $\alpha$, resolving the discrepancy
      between the empirical $\alpha \approx 0.44$ and the structural prediction $\alpha = 1/2$.
